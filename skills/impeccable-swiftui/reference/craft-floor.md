# Craft floor

Load this after the direction is settled, and build without announcing the checklist. A pinned brief or the committed visual world overrides anything here; your own habit does not. When the design hook is active it already enforces the mechanical checks below as you edit (the `(detector: …)` ids): act on its findings instead of re-auditing each rule. Everything without an id is yours to see; no scanner will.

## Verify

Each of these is a check on the built result, not an intention. Run them together in the batched inspection round: one `#Preview` matrix for speed (light, dark, `.dynamicTypeSize(.accessibility3)`, the smallest and largest shipped device), then the Simulator for truth. The checks share one render.

- **Contrast, in both appearances.** Body and placeholder text ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1, in light *and* dark, and still legible with Increase Contrast on (give custom colorsets a High Contrast variant). Hierarchy comes from `.foregroundStyle(.secondary)` / `.tertiary` and semantic colors, not opacity on a hard-coded color. On a colored surface, tint secondary text from that hue or use the foreground's own hierarchy; never a gray. No pure `#000`/`#FFF` surfaces as the brand canvas; the system backgrounds already pick the right near-extremes. (detector: `gray-on-color`, `opacity-hierarchy`, `pure-black-white-surface`, `hardcoded-color`)
- **Depth.** A shadow carries a y-offset and a soft radius, and is quiet: `.shadow(color: .black.opacity(0.08), radius: 12, y: 4)` territory. A zero-offset colored halo is decoration. In dark mode elevation comes from lighter surfaces (`secondarySystemBackground`, grouped backgrounds, materials), not stronger shadows. Declare elevation once: a hairline stroke under a wide soft shadow is the ghost card. (detector: `glow-shadow`)
- **Spacing.** One scale on a 4/8 pt grid, held in named constants. Tight groups, generous separation, more space above a section title than below it. Respect the system's margins (`List`/`Form` insets, `.contentMargins`, `.safeAreaPadding`, layout margins on iPad and Mac) instead of hand-padding every edge. Read the values in code, not by eye.
- **Type.** Every string on a text style (`.largeTitle` … `.caption2`) or a custom face with `Font.custom(_:size:relativeTo:)`; spacing and icon sizes that sit with text scale through `@ScaledMetric`. Run the real copy at `accessibility3` and the largest AX size: rows reflow (swap `HStack` for `VStack` on `dynamicTypeSize.isAccessibilitySize`, or `ViewThatFits`), essential text never truncates, `.minimumScaleFactor` is for chrome only. Caption2 (11 pt) is the floor. Prose holds a 65–75 character measure on iPad and Mac; cap it with `.frame(maxWidth:)`. Obvious steps in size and weight. Don't add negative `.tracking` to SF: it already tracks optically per size; a bundled display face may take -0.02 to -0.03 em, never tighter than -0.04. Changing numbers get `.monospacedDigit()`. (detector: `fixed-font-size`, `custom-font-no-relative`, `tiny-font`)
- **Motion.** One authored moment, not scattered effects and not the same entrance on every section. Springs, not curves: `.smooth`, `.snappy`, or `.spring(duration:bounce:)` with bounce ≤ 0.15 unless the subject literally bounces; SwiftUI springs retarget when interrupted, so let them. Animate from an already-visible default; content that fades in from zero on appear reads as loading. Reach past opacity and offset: `matchedGeometryEffect`, `.navigationTransition(.zoom(sourceID:in:))` (iOS 18+), `.contentTransition(.numericText())`, `.symbolEffect`, `.scrollTransition`, `.visualEffect`, blur, mask, and material all belong to the palette. Every authored moment has a Reduce Motion path (crossfade or none) read from `@Environment(\.accessibilityReduceMotion)`. (detector: `bounce-easing`, `section-fade`, `reduce-motion-ignored`)
- **States.** Pressed (a `ButtonStyle` reading `configuration.isPressed`, feedback on the control, not an image inside it), disabled, selected, focused (keyboard on iPad and Mac, Digital Crown and focus on watch and visionOS), loading (`.redacted(reason: .placeholder)` on the real layout; a `ProgressView` only in place of the action that is running), error (inline, recoverable), empty (`ContentUnavailableView` on iOS 17+ with a next action, not "Nothing here"). Plus real content: long names, German and Finnish lengths, right-to-left, zero, one, and ten thousand items; working controls; size-class-driven composition.
- **Access.** Image-only buttons carry an `accessibilityLabel`; tappable things are `Button`s, not `.onTapGesture`; targets are 44×44 pt (60 pt on visionOS) via `.frame(minWidth:minHeight:)` or `.contentShape`. Group cards into one element (`.accessibilityElement(children: .combine)`), mark headings (`.accessibilityAddTraits(.isHeader)`), keep VoiceOver order equal to reading order, never convey state by color alone. Flip Reduce Transparency and Differentiate Without Color once. (detector: `icon-button-no-label`, `tap-gesture-as-button`, `small-touch-target`)
- **System surfaces.** The parts you did not draw still carry the design, and they ship with defaults that belong to no design system. The app tint (the `AccentColor` asset, `.tint`) is set on purpose. Copyable content allows `.textSelection(.enabled)`. Every field has its `.keyboardType`, `.textContentType` (`.emailAddress`, `.oneTimeCode`, `.newPassword` for AutoFill), `.submitLabel`, and `.onSubmit` that advances `@FocusState` to the next field; `.scrollDismissesKeyboard(.interactively)` on forms. Scroll views decide their indicators and `.scrollBounceBehavior(.basedOnSize)`. Remote data gets `.refreshable`. Rows get `.swipeActions` and `.contextMenu` (with a `preview:` where a preview helps); destructive actions carry `role: .destructive`. Haptics are paired to state with `.sensoryFeedback(_:trigger:)` (success, selection, impact), never sprayed. Alerts and `.confirmationDialog` use specific verbs. The app icon has light, dark, and tinted variants (iOS 18+; layered Icon Composer icons on iOS 26+); the launch screen's background matches the first frame so launch doesn't flash. The status bar reads over full-bleed art. This is the cheapest signal that an app was built rather than assembled, and the one models skip most reliably.
- **Copy.** The product's own language. Buttons name their action ("Save Draft", not "OK"); errors name the problem and the recovery; permission prompts say what the person gets. Title case for buttons and titles per HIG, sentence case for body.
- **Coverage.** Every brief requirement present and findable within seconds, on every shipped device class.

## Refuse

These are the category's defaults, not bans: the brief's own words can earn any of them. Reaching for one when the axis is free means you were not deciding; recognizing that means rewriting the element, not softening it.

Screen scaffolds:

- A stack of same-size cards (icon, heading, line of text) as the screen's structure. Cards are the lazy container; a `List`, `Form`, or sectioned layout is usually what the content wanted. Nested cards are always wrong.
- The hero-metric template: big number, small label, row of supporting stats, accent. Dashboards earn a number's size from its importance, not from the template.
- A kicker or eyebrow (uppercase, tracked caption) above a title. This one is a ban, not a default: no brief earns it back. The title carries its own weight. System section headers are the platform's, not an eyebrow. (detector: `eyebrow-label`)
- Section numbers ("01", "02") unless the sequence itself carries information the person needs. (detector: `numbered-section`)
- A sheet, full-screen cover, or alert for a task that needs neither interruption nor protected focus. Inline, a pushed view, a popover, or an undoable action comes first.
- A custom navigation bar or back button replacing the system one. It breaks edge-swipe back, large-title collapse, and the toolbar's glass. (detector: `hidden-nav-bar`)

Surface habits:

- Gradient text. Emphasis comes from weight, size, or the tint. (detector: `gradient-text`)
- The AI palette: purple-to-blue, indigo-to-pink, cyan glows. (detector: `ai-gradient-palette`)
- Glass and materials as decoration. Materials and Liquid Glass (`glassEffect`, `GlassEffectContainer`, `.buttonStyle(.glass)`, iOS 26+) belong to the system layer that floats over content: bars, toolbars, floating controls, sheets. Content cards stay opaque. (detector: `glass-by-default`)
- A colored leading stripe on cards, rows, callouts, or alerts. Use an SF Symbol, the tint, or the row's own structure. (detector: `side-stripe`)
- Hard offset shadows (radius 0, offset 4) outside a world that actually chose neobrutalism. A costume, not a depth system. (detector: `hard-offset-shadow`)
- Pill-shaped cards. Card corners stay 10–20 pt with `.continuous` style, concentric with their container (`ContainerRelativeShape`, `ConcentricRectangle` on iOS 26+); capsules are for small controls. (detector: `over-rounded`)
- Gauges, progress rings, sparklines, and soft rounded rectangles standing in for content.
- `.fontDesign(.monospaced)` as a costume for "technical" rather than for code, data, or measurement.
- A display voice approximated. When the world pinned a display face, bundle it (`UIAppFonts` / `ATSApplicationFontsPath`) and scale it with `relativeTo:`; the closest installed font is a failure, not a fallback. When it didn't, SF's own axes (`.fontWidth(.expanded)`, `.fontDesign(.rounded)`, New York via `.serif`) are a deliberate choice, not a default. Brand faces stay out of labels, buttons, and data. (detector: `overused-font`)
- Emoji or Unicode glyphs standing in for an icon system. SF Symbols, or custom symbols exported from the SF Symbols template, in one weight and rendering mode. (detector: `emoji-icon`)
- Geometric masks standing in for organic contours: a `Circle` clip approximating a photographed subject's edge. Lift the subject for real (Vision's `VNGenerateForegroundInstanceMaskRequest`, iOS 17+) or ship a cut-out asset.
- Illustration that imitates drawing: sketchy vectors, fake paper grain, stripe or grid backgrounds with nothing measured on them. Real illustration or none; crisp shapes, diagrams, `Canvas`, and shaders stay first-class.
- Light or dark picked by category, or forced app-wide with `.preferredColorScheme`. Both appearances ship; which one the hero screens are composed for comes from the use scene: who, where, under what light.
- Claims, prices, ratings, and configuration invented for effect. They come from supplied truth; label illustrative values honestly.
- Layout from `UIScreen.main.bounds` or a fixed phone width centered on iPad and Mac. Size from the container. (detector: `screen-bounds-layout`)
- Deprecated API as a style choice: `NavigationView`, `.foregroundColor`, `.cornerRadius`, `.animation(_:)` without `value:`. (detector: `deprecated-api`)

The floor holds the mechanics; it never picks the direction. With every check green, spend the screen on the committed world, and when torn between refined and committed, commit.
